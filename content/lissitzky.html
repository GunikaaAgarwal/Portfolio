01 / The brief

How can a publication
express its subject?

For Design Through the Decades, I explored El Lissitzky’s influence on visual communication. The challenge was to turn research about his geometric language, structural rigor, and social purpose into a publication with its own visual identity.

I wanted the design to do more than frame the article. Scale, contrast, and the relationship between type and image could carry the story, while a consistent grid kept the research approachable.

The design challenge

Expressive enough to reflect the work.
Clear enough to invite reading.

02 / Research into form

From historical context
to design decisions.

The research connected Lissitzky’s graphic work, Proun compositions, and exhibition spaces to a wider story about visual communication. Geometric abstraction and structural thinking became the starting points for the magazine’s visual language.

I translated those themes into strong contrasts, shifts in scale, and recurring geometric forms. The goal was a contemporary interpretation that supported the article’s narrative.

Cover exploration

Different compositions.
One editorial voice.

I explored several cover directions, moving between portrait-led compositions and more abstract arrangements of type and geometry. Each direction tested a different balance of image, negative space, and visual emphasis.

The final cover brings bold typography and geometric forms together, establishing a language that continues through the interior spreads.

03 / Building the visual system

A grid to hold it together.
Space to break the rhythm.

With the grid and type system established, I iterated on key spreads, testing scale, white space, image placement, and text structure. Critique feedback helped me simplify dense sections and improve scanning patterns.

I then refined color accents, typographic treatments, and recurring layout motifs. The system moves between bold, expressive spreads and minimal, text-led pages without losing the publication’s identity.

Outcome & reflection

Research, made
into a visual system.

The result is a finished publication that connects research, cover design, and interior layouts through one editorial system. The final work demonstrates how a grid can support both expressive form and functional clarity.

This project strengthened my approach to grid design, typographic rhythm, and storytelling. A next step would be to test an interactive adaptation with readers and learn how the same visual system behaves across digital formats.
