01 / Project overview

A sales reference,
with a lot to carry.

ABPAL’s catalog presents electrical products and sourcing support for industrial, commercial, and residential projects. The publication covers the company story, partner brands, product categories, applications, infrastructure, and service information.

My contribution was the catalog design: shaping varied business and product content into a coherent editorial system. Unlike a self-directed publication, the visual language needed to serve an existing business and keep practical information easy to find.

02 / The reader’s task

Real buying questions.
Clear places to look.

The catalog’s content points to three practical use cases. These frame the design decisions below, rather than representing findings from a separate user study.

01 / Procurement

Find the right range.

Buyers need a route into relevant categories and brands before narrowing a sourcing conversation.

02 / Technical selection

Understand the application.

Technical readers need product descriptions and application information that can be scanned without losing context.

03 / Sales & support

Know what happens next.

Customer-facing conversations need a shared reference for services, stock availability, order details, and support.

The communication challenge

Show the breadth of the business.
Keep the next answer easy to find.

03 / Information architecture

Give a broad offering
a clear way in.

The publication moves from company context and a brand directory into product ranges, followed by service and business information. A numbered contents page gives readers an overview of the range and a direct reference to the section they need.

This structure supports both an introductory read and a targeted lookup during a business conversation.

Product-page design

Make technical variety
feel organized.

Product names, short descriptions, images, and application groups create different entry points into the information. Repeated layouts make the content easier to compare visually while leaving room for different kinds of product information.

For the lighting range, a visual grid groups recognizable product types with concise descriptions. The cable page uses distinct application blocks to connect the range with industrial, commercial, residential, and specialized needs.

{{figure:abpal-catalog-products|Cable information — descriptions and application groups}}{{figure:abpal-catalog-lighting|Lighting range — image-led product groupings and short descriptions}}

Visual consistency

One business.
One publication system.

Deep blueYellow accentsWhite space

Deep blue, yellow accents, recurring angled forms, and consistent footer elements connect the interior pages to ABPAL’s identity. Headings and visual groupings separate dense content into manageable sections.

The customer-care page extends this system beyond products. Support, genuine products, training, and order information help the catalog communicate the broader service offering.

Outcome

67 pages.
One shared reference.

The delivered catalog brings company information, brands, product ranges, and customer support into one publication. Its content hierarchy and repeated visual patterns provide a consistent reference for product and sales communication.

The observable outcome is a completed, publicly available business catalog. Its commercial effectiveness could be evaluated through time-to-find tasks, sales-team feedback, and the quality of customer inquiries; these are future evaluation opportunities, not measured results from this project.
